9-LINE MEDEVAC REQUEST

Leader's Book
HOW TO USE
  1. Treat first. Stop life-threatening bleeding, protect the airway, treat the casualty. Have your RTO or another Soldier send the request while care continues.
  2. Fill in the blanks below. Transmit lines 1–5 first; lines 6–9 can follow as time allows.
  3. Send on the designated net using the authentication/encryption required by your unit SOP. Have the receiver read back lines 1 through 5.

SALUTE REPORT

Leader's Book
HOW TO USE
  • Report what you SEE, not what you think. Be specific and use numbers.
  • Send it as soon as possible, then send updates as the situation changes.
  • Report in order: S-A-L-U-T-E. If you do not know an item, say “unknown” — do not guess.

LACE REPORT

Leader's Book
HOW TO USE
  • Leaders send a LACE (also called a quick status report) to higher after contact or during reorganization, and any time the commander asks for status.
  • Collect the numbers from your team/squad leaders quickly, consolidate, and report in order: L-A-C-E.
  • Use the status-color or percentage method your unit SOP specifies (Green / Amber / Red / Black). Request resupply for anything below your SOP's threshold.

PACE PLAN

Leader's Book
HOW TO USE
  • Primary → Alternate → Contingency → Emergency: four ways to communicate, in the order you will try them.
  • Make each method different (for example: FM voice, SATCOM/second radio, phone/text or messenger, runner/visual signal). If one fails, the others should not fail the same way.
  • Set a trigger for moving to the next method (for example: no response after 2 call attempts or 5 minutes).
  • Brief the PACE plan to every Soldier and rehearse it. Update it any time the mission, terrain, or equipment changes.

TROOP LEADING PROCEDURES

Leader's Book
HOW TO USE
  • TLP is the 8-step process a small-unit leader uses to turn a mission into an executed plan. Steps are not always sequential — several happen at the same time.
  • 1/3 – 2/3 Rule: use no more than one-third of the available time to plan and issue the order; leave two-thirds for subordinates to prepare.
  • Work backward from the start time. Write times in the last column and check each step off.
#StepKey ActionsTime / Done
1Receive the missionRead the order (WARNORD, OPORD, or FRAGORD). Identify the mission, task and purpose, and time available. Make a time schedule.
2Issue a warning orderAlert your Soldiers early: situation, mission, general instructions, when/where the OPORD will be issued, and what they must start preparing now.
3Make a tentative planAnalyze the mission using METT-TC, develop courses of action, compare, and pick one. Update the plan as new information arrives.
4Initiate movementBegin any movement needed to prepare or position the unit (assembly area, recon teams, vehicles, supplies).
5Conduct reconnaissanceConfirm the terrain, enemy, routes, and objective by map, imagery, and on-the-ground recon whenever possible. Update the plan with what you learn.
6Complete the planFinalize the plan with recon results. Prepare the overlay/sketch, task organization, and a clear concept of operations. Confirm coordination with adjacent units.
7Issue the orderBrief the OPORD in the 5-paragraph format, with a terrain model or sketch if possible. Confirm understanding.
8Supervise and refineConduct backbriefs, rehearsals, precombat checks (PCCs), and precombat inspections (PCIs). Check, fix, and adjust. This step continues until execution.

GOTWA – 5-POINT CONTINGENCY PLAN

Leader's Book
HOW TO USE
  • Give a GOTWA to your second-in-command (or the senior Soldier left behind) every time you leave the unit or position — for a recon, a leader's meeting, a patrol, or any time you are away from your Soldiers.
  • Say it out loud, make sure it is understood, and write it down. Update it when you return.
